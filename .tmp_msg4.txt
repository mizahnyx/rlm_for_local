Owner call (2026-09-14): require citations in the prompt first, measure, and add
a refusal only if the requirement does not take. This is the first two thirds.

What was wrong: the live rerun searched twice, read three passages, printed seven
addresses into its own cell output — and submitted 179 characters citing none of
them. The only citation instruction was a clause inside the `corpus_search`
helper's description ("quote its address in your answer"), which is a place a
model under a turn budget skips.

What changed:

- `CORPUS_SECTION_LINES` now carries the requirement as a standing rule of the
  run: every claim about the corpus carries the address it came from, in a final
  `Citations: <path>#L<start>-<end>; …` line, and an answer with no address and
  no coverage note is named as a guess. The helper bullet keeps its output shape
  and loses the buried clause.
- `ADDRESS_IN_TEXT_RE` in `rlm_kernel/textindex.py` is the unanchored form of the
  citation grammar — one place owns what an address looks like, so the harness
  cannot drift from the corpus about it.
- `RootLoop._record_citations` runs at all three points where an answer becomes
  final (answer dict, courtesy `FINAL:`, forced finalization). It counts
  (`corpus_answers`, `corpus_answers_uncited`) and writes one `corpus_citation`
  guardrail event with `answers_with_address=True|False`. It never blocks — that
  is the owner's call, not an omission: on a partly-indexed corpus "found nothing
  citable" and "ignored the instruction" are indistinguishable from outside.
- The check is for an address anywhere in the answer, not for the line's format:
  an answer that quotes its addresses is grounded even if it lays them out
  differently, while a `Citations:` heading with nothing checkable after it is
  not.

Verified: 4 kernel tests on the address shape, a prompt test that the requirement

Two mutation-harness defects found while doing this, both fixed and both recorded
as roadmap CL5 because they are the reason a guard verdict could be wrong:

- **Stale bytecode hid a mutation.** CPython validates a `.pyc` against the
  source's size and its mtime truncated to whole seconds, so a mutation that
  keeps the file the same length and lands in the same second as an earlier
  compile is imported from the old bytecode and looks vacuous. The R23 entry
  shortens `schema_version` to `schema` (7 bytes), exactly as the entry before it
  does — which is why it read VACUOUS in 2 of 3 full runs. Each mutation run now
  compiles into its own `PYTHONPYCACHEPREFIX`; that entry is red 5/5.
- **A coin-flip test.** The RO2 directory-timestamp mutation was caught by a
  walk-vs-index comparison that only usually sees the difference (green about 1
  run in 4). It is now asserted directly — move a directory's mtime, the digest
  must not move — plus a control that moves a file's mtime and requires it to
  move. Red 5/5.
- The table also confirms a green first result once before reporting it (`RERUN`
  is printed and the confirmation is the recorded verdict), because an instrument
  that cries wolf stops being read. `tests/test_guard_nonvacuity.py` (5 tests)
  pins that behaviour rather than a self-mutation: a table that mutates its own
  source mid-run is a new hazard, not a new guard.
